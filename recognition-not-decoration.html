Brand assets · 20 June 2026

The goal of branding is to be recognised.

Looking different every time is not the point. Being recognised every time is.

Levi’s offers a useful lesson in distinctive brand assets. Its familiar red tab, wordmark and product details have been repeated for long enough to carry meaning without needing a new visual idea each time.

That’s what a strong brand asset can do. Even when one element is absent, the wider system can still make the brand recognisable.

The small red tab on the back pocket of a pair of jeans was introduced to help people identify the product. Over time, repetition has made that modest detail a recognisable signal.

Recognition, not decoration

This is where many brands lose focus. There is a temptation to add another colour, graphic, campaign style or layer of creativity. Each addition might feel fresh internally, but it can make recognition harder externally.

The strongest brands are not necessarily those with the most assets. They often have fewer, more memorable cues, repeated consistently over time.

For an established business, the opportunity is often to simplify. Choose the signals worth investing in, strengthen recognition and let familiarity build authority.

Before adding more, ask whether it makes the brand more memorable, or simply gives people more to remember.
