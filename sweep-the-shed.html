Brand values · 7 July 2026

Values should shape behaviour, not decorate a wall.

The values that matter most are not the ones that sound good in a presentation. They are the ones people can recognise, remember and put into practice.

Many businesses have values. Fewer can point to the moment those values changed a decision, raised a standard or made a difficult conversation easier.

That’s the difference between a list of admirable words and a useful operating principle.

A value should tell people what to do

The All Blacks have a phrase: Sweep the Shed. After a match, the biggest names in the team help clean the changing room. It’s a simple instruction with a clear meaning: no one is above the team.

It works because it’s specific. It doesn’t ask people to remember a broad idea such as teamwork or humility. It gives them a behaviour to live.

Generic values are not enough

Honesty, integrity and professionalism matter. But they should be expected, not used as the defining character of a business. They don’t tell a team what makes this organisation different, or how it should behave when the pressure is on.

Strong values make that link. They connect the ambition of the business to the everyday choices people make: how they work with each other, how they serve customers and how they decide what good looks like.

Make values memorable enough to use

A good test is whether someone can repeat your values without looking at a wall or opening a PDF. A better test is whether they would know how to apply them tomorrow.

At i-SPY, values are used as practical prompts: Enjoy the Ride. Look Again. Keep It Human. Sweat the Thinking, Not the Theatre. Find a Better Way. They’re not there simply to describe the business. They’re there to shape it.

For a growing business, that matters. Values can set direction early, make standards visible and give people a shared way to move forward without adding more process or theatre.

The best values don’t just sit on a wall. They create stories people can live.

So the question isn’t whether your business has values. It’s whether they influence behaviour in a way people can see.
