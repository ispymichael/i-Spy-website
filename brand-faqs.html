Brand clarity

Brand FAQs

Questions for businesses that know something isn’t working, but aren’t yet sure whether the answer is a rebrand, clearer messaging, a better website or stronger creative direction.

How do we know if we need a rebrand?

Not every business needs a full rebrand. Sometimes the smarter first step is to clarify the proposition, message, audience, positioning or creative direction.

A diagnosis helps separate a genuine identity problem from a clarity or consistency problem before budget is committed.

Should we look at competitors before changing our brand?

Yes, but not to copy them. Looking at competitors shows what the market already says, where businesses are starting to look and sound alike, and where there may be room for your brand to stand apart.

It helps make sure any changes make you more distinctive, not just more polished.

What does brand clarity actually mean?

It means being able to explain who you are for, what you offer, why it matters and why people should choose you. The same decisions should guide your website, marketing, sales materials and creative work.

A simple test is whether your leadership, sales and marketing teams describe the business in broadly the same way without relying on a script.

Why does our website feel unclear even if it looks fine?

The issue is often not design quality. It may be a weak proposition, unclear messaging, poor page hierarchy or a mismatch between what the business now is and how it presents itself.

If visitors can admire the site but still can’t explain what the company does, who it helps or why it’s different, redesigning the surface won’t solve the underlying problem.

What is the difference between brand strategy and design?

Brand strategy defines the direction, audience, proposition, message and role of the brand. Design turns that thinking into a visible and usable system.

Good design can still feel weak when no clear decision is holding it together.

Why does our marketing feel inconsistent?

Inconsistency often comes from unclear messaging, weak brand rules, too many disconnected campaigns or different people interpreting the business in different ways.

The answer’s not always tighter control. Teams may need a smaller set of shared messages and creative principles that help them make consistent decisions without making every piece of work identical.

What should we fix first, brand, website or marketing?

Start by diagnosing the real issue. If the proposition is unclear, a new website may only make the problem look better. If the message is strong but execution is weak, creative direction may be the priority.

Can better brand clarity save wasted marketing spend?

Yes. It helps you write better briefs, avoid cosmetic fixes and spend money on the work that matters most.

Is brand only relevant for larger businesses?

No. Brand is especially important for SMEs because it helps them explain their value clearly, build trust faster and compete without pretending to be something they’re not.

What is a proposition?

A proposition is the clearest expression of what the business offers, who it is for, why it matters and why someone should choose it.

It’s not necessarily a public-facing slogan. It’s the decision underneath the website copy, sales story and marketing brief.

Where should we start if we aren’t sure what the problem is?

Start with a Brand Diagnosis or the i-Spy Brand Check. The aim is to understand what’s working, what’s unclear and what should be fixed first.
